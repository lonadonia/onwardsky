# Surface: Onward Sky homepage, header through "Be ready when proof of onward travel is requested"

Mode: Persuade. Audience: visa applicants, one-way travelers, travelers facing immigration checks, last-minute fliers (see PRODUCT.md). Job: start a route search in the hero booking form and hand it to the booking wizard. Proof on hand: verifiable PNR, auto-expiry, human support, 100+ airlines with nine named examples (the client's own logos), Trustpilot 4.0 and three review excerpts, from $9.99, delivered in 2 minutes. Constraints: form visible above the fold on desktop, every form capability preserved, no invented claims, scope ends with the "Be ready" section, UI colours only from the production palette, Figtree only, artwork only the client's own or taken from open libraries and open data (never drawn by hand), and since round 6 no photograph: the hero is vectors only. Revision history: v1 (cold "enroute chart") and v2 (invented palette, stock illustration, a second typeface) were rejected; v3 kept the client's colours, type and art but also the live site's composition and was rejected as "only small changes"; v4 replaced the composition (a deep-teal sky, two sample itinerary pages behind a boarding-pass form); v5 removed the two pages and used one photograph under brand-teal veils; the client then asked to remove the photograph, make the hero vectors only, and improve the other sections too. v6 is a computed route map on the brand-teal panel, a redesigned reviews section and a refined "Be ready".

## Direction contract

THESIS: The page is one family of tickets, and the hero is a map of where onward tickets go. A dot-matrix world drawn from Natural Earth's open data, with flight routes between real airports, departure-board codes and one airplane in flight, sits at the right of the brand-teal panel; a boarding-pass booking bar spans the panel; one glass bar holds the promises; the airline logos run along the foot like a horizon. Below, the Trustpilot score on a small ticket leads three review tickets (the client's stub, stood upright, with pictures composed from Tabler Icons in the client's construction), and the client's ticket stubs sit on the same dotted map. The panel grows with the screen (to 1640px) and the map runs on to its edge. It refuses the flat form-on-a-gradient hero of the live site, the invented colours of v1 and v2, the re-skin of v3, the clutter of v4's documents and the stock feel of v5's photograph.

OWN-WORLD: Production teal ink (#0E2F34), brand teal (#156970), deep teal (#0F555B, #0F5A60), soft teal surfaces (#E7F3F3, #F5FAFA), aqua (#86D6D2) for the key word, the routes and icons on dark, mint (#C8E6E1) and peach (#FFD3BA) for the illustrations' blocks and reviewers' initials, and one burnt-orange action (#C94300); Figtree only, 800 for the hero, codes and the score, 700 for sections; ticket vocabulary (dashed perforation, semicircle notches, barcode bar, small-caps stub labels, departure-board codes); the client's four illustrations and nine airline logos; Phosphor icons; Natural Earth land as a dot matrix (the client's dotted-globe motif, made precise).

STORY: In one line the visitor learns what they get; the routes and the plane say "flights between real airports" before a word is read; a ticket-shaped form starts the search, with price and delivery on its stub beside the orange action; the three promises and the Trustpilot score follow as one bar; real airline logos drift past as proof; the reviews open with the score itself and three real excerpts with names and dates; then each person finds their own moment (check-in, arrival, visa, take-off) on a ticket stub, with the action beside the heading.

FIRST VIEWPORT: White header (logo, five links, WhatsApp support, orange CTA). An inset rounded brand-teal panel: at the left a three-line 62px white headline with "verifiable" in aqua and the 19px lead on the plain gradient; at the right the route map (Europe to South-East Asia, five dashed aqua routes, seven markers, LHR, DXB, DEL and BKK in glass tags, a white airplane on the Delhi-London route) over a soft aqua glow, masked out across the text column; the trip tabs and, across the full panel width, a boarding-pass bar (white half: label, From | To with swap, travel date, passengers; tinted stub: "From $9.99", "Delivered in 2 minutes", the orange CTA) with two notches and a perforation that show the panel; assurances and payment marks beneath it; one glass proof bar with four cells; and as the panel's foot the darkest teal strip with the nine logos scrolling sideways and a pause button. Below 1180px the ticket becomes a white card; from 900px the copy and the card sit side by side with the card set lower so the map shows above it (only the London tag, since the card covers the rest); below 900px the map is not shown or loaded and the panel is the plain gradient.

FORM: Boarding pass x Onward Sky brand; rebuilt after the client asked for a vector-only hero and better sections; seed:9e5708a9b0030f67. Raises: wayfinding clarity (one message per panel, large labels); itinerary document (the route becomes visible codes and a plotted connector as soon as airports are chosen; the form is literally a ticket); cartography (the map is real geography and real airports, computed, so the picture carries information rather than mood).

FINISH: unreviewed and undocumented is unfinished; this build ends with the finish review, the verdict, DESIGN.md, and every shipping vector carrying its provenance (`assets/SOURCES.md`, `tools/build-route-map.mjs`).

Signature interaction: choosing an airport turns its field into a departure-board entry (code plus city); once both ends are set the connector between them turns from dashed to solid accent-bright and the swap control follows. The airline strip scrolls sideways, pauses on hover, on focus, off screen and with a visible pause button. The Trustpilot ticket is one link whose arrow nudges on hover; the "Be ready" tickets, which are not links, keep still.
Motion grammar: one entrance on load (headline and lead, then the form, then the proof bar slide up without fading; the map fades in behind them), one slow marquee, cards slide in on scroll (transform only), then motion only in answer to input; reduced motion shows final states and a static logo row.
